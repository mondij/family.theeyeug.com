sections:
  - heading: "Before you leave"
    bullets:
      - "Take a photo of your packed suitcases before closing them — if anything goes missing or damaged, you have a record"
      - "Screenshot your confirmations (flights, hotel, car rental) so they're accessible without wifi"
      - "Give a trusted person at home a copy of your itinerary and accommodation details"
      - "If your kids are old enough, let them each pick one thing they're most excited about — it builds anticipation and gives you a guaranteed 'win' to plan around"
  - heading: "On travel days"
    bullets:
      - "Dress kids in layers, regardless of destination climate — vehicles, airports, and restaurants are air-conditioned unpredictably"
      - "Snacks that aren't messy or crumbly travel better than the ones that seem convenient at the store — pouches and wrapped bars beat crackers in a bag"
      - "Build in more transfer time than Google Maps suggests, especially with strollers, car seats, or more than two bags"
      - "A consistent 'travel day' comfort item — the same snack, the same show — gives kids something predictable in an unpredictable day"
  - heading: "Once you've arrived"
    bullets:
      - "Do one low-key activity on the first day rather than diving straight into the main event — everyone travels better after a buffer day, even a short one"
      - "Find the nearest pharmacy and grocery store in the first 24 hours, before you need them"
      - "Keep the first few days' schedule lighter than you think you need to — jet lag and unfamiliar beds affect kids more than adults often expect"
  - heading: "Managing meltdowns on the road"
    body:
      - "Meltdowns happen more on trips, not less — new environments, disrupted sleep, and too much stimulation all add up. The goal isn't preventing every meltdown; it's having a plan for when one happens."
    bullets:
      - "Identify a 'reset' option ahead of time — a quiet corner, a specific snack, a five-minute walk away from the situation"
      - "Lower expectations for the last hour of any long travel day; that's when meltdowns cluster"
      - "A tired, overstimulated kid usually needs less talking and more quiet, not more explanation"
  - heading: "Money and logistics"
    bullets:
      - "Carry a small amount of local cash even in places that are mostly card-based — some smaller vendors and bathrooms still require it"
      - "Set a daily 'good enough' budget rather than tracking every purchase in real time; it reduces trip-long stress"
      - "Keep one card and some cash separate from your main wallet as a backup"
faq:
  - question: "What's the single most useful family travel tip?"
    answer: "Build in more buffer time than feels necessary, everywhere — between activities, before flights, and on the first day of a trip. Almost every stressful moment on a family trip traces back to not enough slack in the schedule."
  - question: "How do you handle a meltdown while traveling?"
    answer: "Have a 'reset' plan ready before you need it — a quiet spot, a specific comfort item, or simply stepping away from the situation for a few minutes. Trying to reason through a meltdown in the moment rarely works as well as removing the stimulation first."
  - question: "How do you keep costs under control on a family trip?"
    answer: "Set a daily spending target rather than tracking every purchase, and build in a 10–15% buffer for the unplanned extras that come up on every trip."
